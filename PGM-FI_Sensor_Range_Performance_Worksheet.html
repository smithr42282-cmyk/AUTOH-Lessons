When a vehicle has a driveability complaint, no high/low voltage DTCs, and good battery voltage, the next step is to find data list values that are out of range. In this lab you will compare sensor values to expected values, read the EWD to identify each circuit, and use voltage drop testing to find wiring problems — without back probing the sealed ECM/PCM connectors.

You will need: assigned vehicle, HDS, Service Information (SIS / EWD), DMM, sensor test harness, and back probe adapters.

TRAINING AIDS (posted in Canvas): This worksheet does not include the training aids. Download them from the FBS12 module page in Canvas and have them open before you start:
• Training Aid A — Making DMM Measurements at Electrical Connectors
• Training Aid B — PGM-FI Expected Values (use the version that matches your vehicle: Turbo, DI Non-Turbo, or Multiport)
• Training Aid C — Reading Advanced Diagnostics and EWD Schematics
SAFETY: Never back probe under-hood ECM/PCM terminals, never force a probe into a connector cavity, and never pierce wire insulation. Review Training Aid A before taking any measurement.

Part D — Check a 3-Wire Sensor Circuit (MAP)
LOCATE: Open FBS12 Training Aid A — Making DMM Measurements at Electrical Connectors (posted in Canvas).

Sensor Input Voltage Drop

1Record the MAP sensor voltage from the HDS Data List.

2Calculate the sensor input voltage drop. Do not round.

Measured voltage at MAP Sensor input terminal
minus (−) Voltage displayed on HDS Data List for MAP Sensor
= Sensor Input Voltage Drop
TEACHING POINT: If the voltage drop on the signal wire between the sensor and the PCM is less than 0.05 V, the signal wire is good. More than 0.05 V means unwanted resistance in the signal wire.

3Is your input voltage drop within acceptable limits?

Sensor Ground Voltage Drop

4Record the exact voltage reading.

TEACHING POINT: If the voltage drop between the sensor ground and the ECM/PCM ground is 0.05 V or less, the sensor ground circuit is good. The ECM/PCM is typically grounded at G101 and G102 — using the battery negative terminal adds at least four extra connection points to your reading.

5Is your sensor ground voltage drop within acceptable limits?

Reference Voltage Drop

6IG ON, engine OFF. Measure voltage between the MAP sensor test harness VCC terminal and G101 or G102. Record the exact reading.

7Is the voltage between 4.95 V and 5.05 V?

NOTE: You would normally only do Questions 8–10 if you measured less than 4.95 V. For training, complete them anyway.

8Unplug the MAP sensor connector and measure voltage again at the VCC terminal. Record the reading.

9Did you get exactly the same voltage as in Question 6?

10Compare Questions 6 and 8. How much voltage is dropping between the PCM and the sensor? Show your math.

TEACHING POINT: If the voltage at a sensor's VCC terminal is too low, either the PCM is not regulating correctly, or high resistance between the PCM and sensor is causing voltage drop. With the sensor unplugged the circuit is open, so the meter shows the regulated voltage the PCM is supplying.

Part E — Check a 2-Wire Sensor Circuit (ECT Sensor 1)
LOCATE: Open FBS12 Training Aid A — Making DMM Measurements at Electrical Connectors (posted in Canvas).

Sensor Input Voltage Drop

1Record the ECT Sensor 1 voltage from the HDS Data List.

2Calculate the sensor input voltage drop. Do not round.

Measured voltage at ECT Sensor 1 input terminal
minus (−) Voltage displayed on HDS Data List for ECT Sensor 1
= Sensor Input Voltage Drop
TEACHING POINT: If the voltage drop on the input wire between the sensor and the PCM is less than 0.05 V, the signal wire is good. More than 0.05 V means unwanted resistance in the signal wire.

3Is your sensor input voltage drop within acceptable limits?

Sensor Ground Voltage Drop

4IG ON, engine OFF. Measure voltage drop between the ECT Sensor 1 test harness ground terminal and G101 or G102. Record the exact reading.

TEACHING POINT: If the voltage drop between the sensor ground and an engine ground is less than 0.05 V, the ground circuit is good. If it is more than 0.05 V, divide the ground circuit to find where the unwanted resistance is.

5Is your sensor ground voltage drop within acceptable limits?

Reference Voltage

To find out whether the PCM is supplying 5 V to the ECT Sensor 1 circuit, measure open-circuit voltage.

6IG ON, engine OFF. Disconnect the test harness at ECT Sensor 1 and measure voltage between the harness input terminal and G101 or G102. Record the exact reading.

7Is the voltage between 4.95 V and 5.05 V?

TEACHING POINT: Even though you are measuring the input circuit, the meter now shows the PCM's regulated (reference) voltage. With the circuit open, no voltage drops across the fixed resistor inside the PCM. The HDS Data List should show the same voltage as your meter.

8If all wiring checks are OK and the sensor value is still out of range, what could be the problem?
