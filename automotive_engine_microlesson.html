What Is an Engine?

An engine is the heart of the vehicle. It converts chemical energy (from fuel) into mechanical energy that powers the wheels.

All modern car engines — gasoline or diesel — are internal combustion engines. This means combustion (burning fuel with air) happens inside the engine to generate power.

Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to identify engine construction materials, classify engines by cylinder count and arrangement, describe the basic combustion cycle, and compare valve train, ignition, cooling, and fuel-type options.
SLIDE 2 OF 12
Engine Construction
Modern Engines — lightweight materials for better fuel efficiency and performance
  • Aluminum — lightweight and heat-resistant.
  • Magnesium — even lighter than aluminum.
  • Fiber-reinforced plastics — used in components to reduce weight.
Older Engines — built for durability, often heavier
  • Cast Iron — very strong but heavy.
  • Steel — durable but less efficient for modern use.
SLIDE 3 OF 12
Main Component: The Cylinder Block
  • The largest, heaviest part of the engine.
  • Holds the cylinders where combustion occurs.
  • Cast with internal passages for oil and coolant, which help:
  • Lubricate moving parts
  • Regulate engine temperature

How the Engine Works
  1. Air and fuel mix inside each cylinder.
  2. The mixture is compressed by the piston.
  3. It's ignited (by spark or compression).
  4. This causes an explosion that drives the piston down.
  5. Pistons turn the crankshaft, creating rotary motion.
  6. This motion powers the wheels via the drivetrain.

SLIDE 10 OF 12
Fun Fact: Honda VTEC
Two Engine Personalities in One
Honda’s VTEC (Variable Valve Timing and Lift Electronic Control) system is legendary in the car world! Introduced in the late 1980s, VTEC allows an engine to change its valve timing for better fuel efficiency at low RPMs and high performance at high RPMs — basically giving drivers two engine personalities in one.

It’s one of the reasons engines like the B16 and K20 platforms are beloved by car enthusiasts worldwide — reliable, high-revving, and full of character!
SLIDE 11 OF 12
Quick Recap
  • An engine converts chemical energy (fuel) into mechanical energy via internal combustion.
  • Modern engines favor lightweight materials (aluminum, magnesium); older engines used cast iron and steel.
  • The cylinder block is the engine's largest component, housing cylinders and internal oil/coolant passages.
  • Engines are classified by cylinder count (3–16) and arrangement (Inline, Boxer, V-Type).
  • The combustion cycle: mix → compress → ignite → drive the piston → turn the crankshaft → power the wheels.
  • Valve trains (OHC/OHV), ignition systems (spark/compression), cooling systems (air/liquid), and fuel types all vary by engine design.
