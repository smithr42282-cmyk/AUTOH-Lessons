How to Read PicoScope Waveforms

Square, sine and injector · Honda PACT · Shoreline Community College

Every PicoScope measurement works the same way: count the boxes, then multiply by what one box is worth. This guide walks through three practice screens: a square wave, a sine wave and a fuel injector.

Before you start: reading the grid

The screen is a grid of 10 boxes up and 10 boxes across. Up is voltage; across is time. A scope shows how voltage changes over time, while a DVOM shows only one moment.

The three-step routine for any screen:
  1. Up: count boxes from the 0 V line and multiply by volts per box.
  2. Across: count boxes for one full cycle and multiply by time per box. That's the period.
  3. Math: frequency (Hz) = 1,000 ÷ period in ms. Duty cycle (%) = on-time ÷ period × 100.
Tip: dividing into 1,000 ms skips the decimals. A 20 ms period gives 1,000 ÷ 20 = 50 Hz.

Screen 1: square wave

Screen 1 reads 5 V, 50 Hz and 25% duty cycle. Settings: ±10 V range = 2 V per box up; Timebase = 5 ms per box across.

1. Peak voltage (read up from the 0 V line)

  • Start at the 0 V line, not the bottom of the screen.
  • Count boxes up to the top of the pulse: 2½ boxes.
  • 2.5 × 2 V = 5 V.

2. Frequency (read across, then flip it)

  • Find the period, the time for one full cycle. Start at one rising edge (5 ms) and go to the next rising edge (25 ms): 4 boxes × 5 ms = 20 ms.
  • Flip it: 1,000 ÷ 20 ms = 50 Hz. That's 50 cycles every second.

3. Duty cycle (what part of the cycle is it on?)

  • On-time: the pulse stays high from 5 ms to 10 ms, 1 box = 5 ms.
  • 5 ms ÷ 20 ms = 0.25 → 25%. It's on 1 box out of every 4, a quarter of the time.

Watch out for

  • Measuring from the bottom of the screen. 0 V is the center line; counting from the bottom gives 15 V instead of 5 V.
  • Using rise-to-fall as the period. That's only the on-time (5 ms) and gives 200 Hz. The period goes from a rising edge to the next rising edge.
  • Forgetting the units. 1 ÷ 20 = 0.05, which isn't 50 Hz. Use 1,000 ÷ period in ms.

Screen 2: sine wave

Screen 2 reads 6 V peak-to-peak and 100 Hz. Settings: ±5 V range = 1 V per box up; Timebase = 2 ms per box across.

A sine wave swings above and below 0 V, so we measure peak-to-peak (V p-p): from the very top to the very bottom.

1. Peak-to-peak voltage (top to bottom)

  • Top of the wave: 3 boxes above the 0 V line = +3 V.
  • Bottom of the wave: 3 boxes below the 0 V line = −3 V.
  • 3 + 3 = 6 boxes × 1 V = 6 V p-p.

2. Frequency (find one full wave, then flip it)

  • One full wave = one hill plus one valley. Start where the wave crosses 0 V going up (0 ms), follow it over the hill and through the valley, and stop at the next upward crossing (10 ms).
  • 5 boxes × 2 ms = 10 ms period.
  • 1,000 ÷ 10 ms = 100 Hz.
  • Shortcut: peak to the next peak (about 2.5 ms to 12.5 ms) gives the same 10 ms. Start anywhere, as long as you finish at the same spot on the next wave.

A sine wave has no on/off, so there's no duty cycle.

Watch out for

  • Measuring only half the wave. 0 ms to 5 ms is just the hill and gives 200 Hz.
  • Giving the peak (3 V) instead of peak-to-peak (6 V). The worksheet asks for the full top-to-bottom swing.
  • Counting from the bottom of the screen. Find the 0 V center line first.

Real-car connection: on a passive (magnetic) wheel speed sensor, the wave gets taller and the waves get closer together as the wheel spins faster.

Screen 3: fuel injector

Screen 3 reads 3 ms on-time and a 60 V spike. Settings: ±100 V range = 20 V per box up; Timebase = 1 ms per box across.

First, know what you're looking at. The scope lead is on the injector's control wire. The injector gets 12–14 V from a relay all the time, and the ECM turns it on by connecting the other side to ground. On this wire, low means on. Left to right, the picture has three parts:

  1. Off (about 14 V): no current flows, so the scope sees battery voltage through the injector coil.
  2. On (0 V): the ECM grounds the wire, current flows, and the injector opens and sprays fuel.
  3. Spike (60 V): the ECM lets go of the ground, the coil's magnetic field collapses, and it kicks out a quick high-voltage spike before settling back to about 14 V.

1. On-time (pulse width)

  • The trace drops to 0 V at 2 ms and shoots up into the spike at 5 ms.
  • 3 boxes × 1 ms = 3 ms.
  • On-time is how long the ECM holds the injector open: longer on-time = more fuel.

2. Spike voltage

  • Start at the 0 V line and count up to the tip of the spike: 3 boxes.
  • 3 × 20 V = 60 V.
  • A strong spike shows a healthy coil. If one injector's spike is much lower than the others, its winding may be shorted.

Bonus: the off level is a little under 1 box up, about 0.7 × 20 V ≈ 14 V, which confirms full battery voltage at the injector.

Watch out for

  • Thinking the high part is "on." On a ground-side signal the high part is off. On-time is the time spent at 0 V.
  • Measuring the spike from 14 V. That gives 46 V. Always measure from the 0 V line.
  • Ending the on-time too late. It ends where the spike starts, not where the voltage settles back to 14 V.

The PicoScope 4225A handles the spike directly on the ±100 V range; no attenuator is needed. The 60 V is a training value: on a real car, compare each injector with the others and with the Service Express spec.

Quick checklist for any capture

  • Read the range and timebase; work out volts per box (range ÷ 5) and time per box.
  • Find the 0 V center line before counting anything.
  • Decide what kind of signal it is: on/off (square or injector) or swinging (sine).
  • Measure one full cycle, edge to the same edge on the next cycle.
  • Do the math: 1,000 ÷ period (ms) = Hz; on-time ÷ period × 100 = duty %.
  • Ask: on a ground-side part, is "on" the low part?
