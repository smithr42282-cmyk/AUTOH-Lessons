Quick Reference read before you start

Secondary ignition (per cylinder)

  • Firing line: the voltage needed to jump the plug gap (kV). Higher resistance in the gap or the secondary circuit makes it taller.
  • Spark line: the voltage that keeps the spark burning. Its length is the burn time (ms).
  • Coil oscillations: energy left in the coil rings down after the spark ends.
  • Dwell: the time the primary circuit is switched on to charge the coil.
  • Rule of thumb: the coil only has so much energy. A taller firing line usually comes with a shorter burn time.

Injector (saturated, ground-side control)

  • Supply voltage: with the injector off, you see system voltage on the control wire.
  • Turn-on: the ECM driver pulls the circuit to ground and the injector opens.
  • Pulse width: how long the circuit is held at ground (ms). This is the fuel command.
  • Inductive spike: the coil's collapsing field when the driver turns off. A low spike can point to shorted windings.
  • Current ramp: on a low-amp probe, current rises and shows a small pintle hump as the injector opens.
Honda/Acura note: Most current engines use coil-on-plug coils with the igniter built into the coil, so there's no primary winding terminal to scope. View secondary with a COP pickup probe. At the coil connector you can scope the ECM's ignition control signal. Look up pins, wire colors and specs in Service Express / iN.
Safety: Never connect a scope lead directly to secondary ignition. Use a rated COP/secondary pickup only. Keep hands and leads clear of belts and fans while the engine runs.
