Inspecting Honda Electronic Ignition Systems

Honda PACT — Quick-Reference Micro-Lesson

Honda has used two very different ignition architectures over the years. Knowing which one you're looking at — and what's actually inside it — determines how you inspect and test it.

Distributor-Based Ignition (1980s–90s D, B, F, H-series)

A distributor houses a pickup coil (or optical/Hall sensor) that tells the igniter when to fire. The igniter switches an external ignition coil, and the distributor cap/rotor route secondary voltage out to plug wires for each cylinder.

  • Distributor — cap, rotor, pickup coil/crank signal generator
  • Igniter unit — power transistor switching primary current, often mounted on/near the distributor
  • Ignition coil — external, separately testable primary & secondary windings
  • Spark plug wires — carry secondary voltage from cap to each plug

Inspection checklist:

  1. Visually check cap & rotor for cracks, carbon tracking, worn contacts
  2. Inspect plug wires for cracked insulation; measure resistance end-to-end
  3. Ohm the coil's primary and secondary windings against spec
  4. Check pickup coil/sensor air gap and resistance (if applicable)
  5. Confirm igniter triggers coil — swap-test or scope the trigger signal

Coil-On-Plug (COP) (modern Hondas)

No distributor and no plug wires. One small coil sits directly over each spark plug, with the igniter built into the coil itself. The ECM/PCM triggers each coil individually and can detect which cylinder's circuit has a fault.

  • Coil assembly — primary/secondary windings, iron core, and igniter, all sealed as one unit per cylinder
  • Boot & spring — carries secondary voltage the short distance to the plug
  • Connector — switched power, ground, and ECM/PCM trigger (often with a feedback signal)
  • ECM/PCM — commands dwell and firing time per cylinder

Inspection checklist:

  1. Visually check boots for cracks, carbon tracking, heat damage
  2. Inspect connector/terminals for corrosion or a loose pin
  3. Pull DTCs/freeze frame — specific-cylinder misfire or coil-circuit code?
  4. Swap-test the suspect coil to another cylinder
  5. Confirm with scan tool live data and/or a primary/secondary waveform

At a Glance: Key Differences

FeatureDistributor-Based (D/B/F/H-series)Coil-On-Plug (Modern)
Number of coilsOne, shared by all cylindersOne per cylinder
Igniter locationSeparate unit, on/near distributorBuilt into each coil
Secondary voltage pathCap & rotor → plug wiresDirectly from coil to plug (no wires)
Timing controlMechanical/optical trigger in distributor, some ECU inputFully ECM/PCM-controlled per cylinder
Typical fault isolationCap/rotor/wire inspection, coil ohm testDTC per cylinder, swap test, waveform
Safety: Secondary ignition voltage can exceed 20,000–40,000V on either system. Never handle a coil, wire, cap, or spark tester with the engine running or cranking.
