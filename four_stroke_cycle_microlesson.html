Understanding a Stroke

A stroke is one full movement of the piston inside a cylinder — either up or down. In a four-stroke internal combustion engine, the piston completes four strokes to finish one cycle.

QuantityValue
Crankshaft revolutions per cycle2 complete revolutions (720° total)
Crankshaft rotation per stroke180°
Key Point
This cycle happens over and over in each cylinder as long as the engine is running.
Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to name the four strokes in order, describe piston direction and valve status during each stroke, and explain why only one stroke actually produces power.
SLIDE 2 OF 10
The Four Strokes in Detail
1 Intake Stroke

Purpose: To fill the cylinder with fresh air (or air-fuel mixture in older engines).

How It Works
  • The piston begins at Top Dead Center (TDC) and moves downward toward Bottom Dead Center (BDC).
  • The intake valve opens, while the exhaust valve remains closed.
  • As the piston descends, it increases the volume in the cylinder, lowering the pressure inside.
  • Atmospheric pressure forces air into the cylinder through the open intake valve.
  • In naturally aspirated engines, this is a vacuum effect. In turbocharged/supercharged engines, pressurized air is forced in.
Key Point
The more air that enters, the better the power output. Engines are designed to maximize airflow during this stroke for efficiency.
SLIDE 3 OF 10
The Four Strokes in Detail
2 Compression Stroke

Purpose: To compress the air (or air-fuel mixture) into a smaller space, increasing temperature and pressure for efficient combustion.

How It Works
  • The piston moves upward from BDC to TDC.
  • Both intake and exhaust valves are closed.
  • The air inside the cylinder is trapped and compressed by the rising piston.
  • The compression makes the air hotter and denser, preparing it for ignition.
  • In gasoline engines, fuel may be injected before or during this stroke.
  • In diesel engines, only air is compressed; fuel is injected just before ignition.
Key Point
The compression ratio (volume at BDC vs. volume at TDC) is critical. Higher ratios = better power and fuel economy, but require better fuel to avoid knocking.
SLIDE 4 OF 10
The Four Strokes in Detail
3 Power Stroke (Expansion Stroke)

Purpose: To generate the actual power that turns the crankshaft and drives the vehicle.

How It Works
  • The piston is at TDC, and both valves remain closed.
  • In gasoline engines, a spark plug ignites the compressed air-fuel mixture.
  • In diesel engines, the heat from compression ignites the injected fuel.
  • The explosion rapidly expands gases inside the cylinder.
  • This creates high pressure, forcing the piston downward to BDC.
  • The force is transmitted via the connecting rod to the crankshaft, turning it.
Key Point
This is the only stroke that produces power. The rest support it.
SLIDE 5 OF 10
The Four Strokes in Detail
4 Exhaust Stroke

Purpose: To remove burned exhaust gases from the cylinder, preparing it for the next cycle.

How It Works
  • The piston moves upward from BDC to TDC again.
  • The exhaust valve opens, and the intake valve remains closed.
  • Burned gases, still under pressure, begin to escape through the exhaust valve.
  • The rising piston pushes out the remaining exhaust gas.
  • Near the end of this stroke, a slight valve overlap occurs: the intake valve opens as the exhaust valve is closing — helping improve airflow for the next cycle.
Key Point
Efficient removal of exhaust gases prevents leftover gases from diluting the next intake charge.

Extra Notes
  • Flywheel or Torque Converter: keeps the engine spinning between strokes.
  • The cycle repeats continuously in every cylinder while the engine is running.
  • Timing and valve control are critical for performance and efficiency.

Fun Fact: Honda's F1 Legacy
900+ Horsepower From 1.5 Liters
Honda’s F1 racing engines in the late 1980s were so powerful and efficient that the RA168E V6 turbo engine produced over 900 horsepower from a tiny 1.5-liter engine — that’s over 600 hp per liter!

This technology influenced many of Honda’s production engine designs, especially VTEC systems.
SLIDE 9 OF 10
Quick Recap
  • One full cycle = 4 strokes = 2 crankshaft revolutions = 720° of rotation (180° per stroke).
  • Intake (down): intake valve open, exhaust closed — draws in air/fuel.
  • Compression (up): both valves closed — compresses the charge, raising heat and pressure.
  • Power (down): both valves closed — ignition drives the piston down; the only stroke that produces power.
  • Exhaust (up): exhaust valve open, intake closed — expels burned gases, with slight valve overlap at the end.
  • The flywheel/torque converter keeps the engine spinning smoothly between power strokes.

Knowledge Check
10 questions — select an answer, then reveal to check yourself
Question 1
How many crankshaft revolutions does one complete four-stroke cycle require?
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: B
One full four-stroke cycle takes 2 complete crankshaft revolutions, or 720° total — 180° per stroke.
Question 2
During the Intake stroke, what is the status of the valves?
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: C
During the Intake stroke, the intake valve is open and the exhaust valve remains closed, allowing air (or air-fuel mixture) into the cylinder.
Question 3
Which direction does the piston move during the Compression stroke?
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: B
During Compression, the piston moves upward from BDC (Bottom Dead Center) to TDC (Top Dead Center), compressing the trapped air/fuel.
Question 4
In a diesel engine, what ignites the fuel during the Power stroke?
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: B
Diesel engines rely on compression ignition: fuel is injected and ignited by the heat generated from compressing the air, with no spark plug involved.
Question 5
Which of the four strokes is the only one that actually produces power?
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: C
The Power (Expansion) stroke is the only stroke that produces power — the other three strokes support this process.
Question 6
What does a higher compression ratio generally provide, and what does it require?
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: A
Higher compression ratios generally provide better power and fuel economy, but require higher-quality fuel to avoid engine knocking.
Question 7
During the Exhaust stroke, what happens to the valves?
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: C
During the Exhaust stroke, the exhaust valve opens (intake remains closed) to let burned gases escape as the piston rises.
Question 8
What is “valve overlap,” and when does it occur?
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: B
Valve overlap is when the intake valve opens just as the exhaust valve is closing, near the end of the Exhaust stroke, helping improve airflow for the next intake cycle.
Question 9
What keeps the engine spinning smoothly between power strokes?
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: B
The flywheel (or torque converter) stores rotational energy and keeps the engine spinning smoothly between the intermittent power strokes.
Question 10
Approximately how much horsepower per liter did Honda's RA168E F1 engine produce?
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: C
The Honda RA168E V6 turbo produced over 900 horsepower from just 1.5 liters — more than 600 horsepower per liter.
