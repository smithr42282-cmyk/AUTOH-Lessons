What Is Oil Viscosity?

Viscosity is how thick or thin an oil is, and it directly affects how well that oil flows through an engine.

  • Thicker oil — has a higher viscosity and flows slower.
  • Thinner oil — has a lower viscosity and flows faster.
Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to read an SAE oil viscosity rating, explain what the “W” designation means, and describe the difference between single- and multi-viscosity oils.
SLIDE 2 OF 8
The SAE Viscosity Rating System

Oil viscosity is designated by a numbering system devised by the Society of Automotive Engineers (SAE). The higher the number, the higher the viscosity — and the thicker the oil.

SAE WeightRelative Thickness
20-weightThinner
30-weightMedium — thicker than 20, thinner than 40
40-weightThicker
Key Concept
A 30-weight oil is thicker than a 20-weight oil but thinner than a 40-weight oil — the number is a direct scale of thickness.
SLIDE 3 OF 8
The “W” Winter Rating

The W on a multi-viscosity oil label stands for Winter, and it indicates how well the oil performs in cold weather.

Example: 0W-20
This label means the oil performs like a 0-weight oil when cold and like a 20-weight oil when hot.
SLIDE 4 OF 8
How Temperature Affects Oil
  • Oil naturally becomes thicker at lower temperatures.
  • Oil naturally becomes thinner at higher temperatures.
  • A good multi-viscosity oil is engineered to resist this swing so it protects the engine across a wide temperature range.
SLIDE 5 OF 8

SLIDE 6 OF 8
Quick Recap
  • Viscosity = how thick or thin an oil is, and how fast it flows.
  • The SAE numbering system rates viscosity — higher number = thicker oil.
  • The W rating shows cold-weather (Winter) performance; a 0W-20 oil acts like 0-weight cold and 20-weight hot.
  • Oil thickens in the cold and thins in the heat — multi-viscosity oil is designed to manage that swing.
  • Single-viscosity oil suits one temperature range; multi-viscosity oil protects across both ends.
