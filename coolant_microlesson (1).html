Why Coolant Matters

Automotive coolant — also called antifreeze — is a vital fluid that regulates engine temperature, prevents overheating, and protects engine components from corrosion and freezing.

It works by circulating through the engine and radiator, absorbing heat from hot metal surfaces and carrying that heat away to be released into the outside air.

Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to identify the three main coolant technologies, explain how coolant chemistry affects boiling point, and describe coolant’s four primary functions in a vehicle.

Why It Matters on the Job
Mixing incompatible coolant technologies can cause gel formation, clogged passages, and accelerated corrosion. Always verify the OEM-specified coolant before a top-off or flush.

Chemical Properties & Boiling Point

Coolant is typically made of ethylene glycol or propylene glycol, mixed with water and additives.

  • Ethylene glycol — more common, excellent thermal properties, but toxic.
  • Propylene glycol — less common, more environmentally friendly.
ConditionApproximate Boiling Point
Undiluted ethylene glycol~387°F (197°C)
50/50 mix with water~223°F (106°C)
Pressurized system (15 psi radiator cap)Up to ~265°F (129°C)
Key Concept
The elevated boiling point under pressure is crucial: modern engines run hot, and a properly mixed, pressurized coolant system keeps the fluid from evaporating or boiling off exactly when the engine needs it most.
SLIDE 4 OF 8
Primary Uses in Vehicles
  • Temperature Regulation — keeps the engine within its optimal operating range.
  • Freeze Protection — prevents the coolant from freezing in cold climates.
  • Corrosion Inhibition — protects internal engine surfaces from rust and mineral buildup.
  • Lubrication — helps lubricate water pump seals and other wetted components.
SLIDE 5 OF 8
Cool Fact
Coolant technology beyond Earth
Did You Know?
NASA uses a form of glycol coolant in spacecraft to regulate temperatures in space — showing just how critical coolant technology is, even beyond Earth’s atmosphere!

Coolant’s ability to absorb and carry away heat is a direct application of heat transfer principles — conduction, convection, and the specific heat capacity of the coolant mixture.

Quick Recap
  • Three coolant technologies: IAT, OAT, HOAT — each with its own color, chemistry, and service interval.
  • Coolant is a glycol/water/additive mix; ethylene glycol is more common, propylene glycol is more eco-friendly.
  • A 50/50 mix boils around 223°F, but a pressurized 15 psi system pushes that up to about 265°F.
  • Coolant's four jobs: temperature regulation, freeze protection, corrosion inhibition, and lubrication.

Question 1
Which coolant technology is typically green in color and used in pre-1990s vehicles?
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: B
IAT (Inorganic Additive Technology) is commonly green, used in older vehicles, and relies on silicates and phosphates for corrosion protection.
Question 2
GM’s Dex-Cool® is a well-known example of which coolant technology?
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: C
Dex-Cool® is a popular OAT (Organic Acid Technology) coolant, typically orange, red, or dark green, and rated for up to 5 years / 150,000 miles.
Question 3
Approximately what is the boiling point of a 50/50 ethylene glycol and water mixture (unpressurized)?
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: C
A 50/50 mix of ethylene glycol and water boils at approximately 223°F (106°C) at atmospheric pressure.
Question 4
A typical 15 psi radiator cap raises the coolant's effective boiling point to approximately:
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: D
Pressurizing the system to 15 psi raises the boiling point to roughly 265°F (129°C), helping prevent boil-over under high engine temperatures.
Question 5
Which of the following is NOT one of coolant’s four primary functions?
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: B
Coolant’s four primary functions are temperature regulation, freeze protection, corrosion inhibition, and lubrication. Fuel atomization is a fuel-system function, unrelated to coolant.
Question 6
Which glycol type is more environmentally friendly, though less commonly used than the alternative?
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: B
Propylene glycol is more environmentally friendly than ethylene glycol, though ethylene glycol remains more common due to its superior thermal properties.
Question 7
How often should a typical IAT coolant be replaced?
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: C
IAT coolant has the shortest service life of the three technologies discussed — typically 2–3 years or 30,000 miles.
Question 8
HOAT (Hybrid Organic Acid Technology) coolant is usually which color(s)?
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: C
HOAT coolant is commonly yellow, turquoise, or pink, and is common in European and some American vehicles.
Question 9
True or False: NASA uses a form of glycol coolant to help regulate temperatures in spacecraft.
▸ Check Your Answer
Correct Answer: A
True — glycol-based coolant technology is used in spacecraft thermal regulation, demonstrating how critical this chemistry is even outside Earth’s atmosphere.
