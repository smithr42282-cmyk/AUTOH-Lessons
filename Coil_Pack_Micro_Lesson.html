How a Honda COP Coil Works read before you start

Honda gasoline engines use coil-on-plug (COP) ignition: one coil sits directly on each spark plug, with no distributor and no plug wires. On most Honda COP coils the igniter (driver transistor) is built into the coil.

What's inside the coil

  • Primary winding: a few hundred turns of heavier wire. The igniter switches current through it to build a magnetic field.
  • Secondary winding: thousands of turns of fine wire around the same iron core. It makes the high voltage for the plug.
  • Iron core: laminated steel that concentrates the magnetic field.
  • Igniter: switches the primary on and off when the ECM/PCM commands it.
  • Boot and spring: carry secondary voltage to the plug. The boot is the most common failure point.
  • Connector: switched power, ground and the ECM/PCM trigger signal.
  • Retaining bolt: holds the coil fully seated; a loose coil can cause an intermittent misfire.

How the ECM/PCM fires the coil

  1. The ECM/PCM sends the trigger (dwell) signal; the igniter grounds the primary and a magnetic field builds.
  2. At the commanded ignition timing, the ECM/PCM ends the signal and the igniter turns the primary off.
  3. The field collapses quickly and induces tens of thousands of volts in the secondary, firing the plug.

Dwell is how long the primary stays on (how much energy the coil stores). Ignition timing is when the coil fires, based on crank and cam position, load, RPM and knock feedback.

Symptoms and codes

  • Rough idle or a miss, often worse hot or in damp weather (a cracked boot can carbon-track).
  • P0301–P0306 misfire on a specific cylinder · P0351–P0356 ignition coil A–F circuit.
  • P0300 random misfire: more than one cylinder, which usually points to a shared cause (fuel, vacuum leak, wiring or ground), not one coil.

Test sequence

  1. Visual: boot cracks or carbon tracking, connector and terminals, oil in the plug well, the spark plug itself, coil seated and bolt torqued.
  2. DTCs and freeze frame (HDS): which cylinder, and under what conditions (RPM, load, temperature).
  3. Swap test: swap the suspect coil with another cylinder's coil, clear codes, run it. Misfire follows the coil = bad coil. Misfire stays = look at the plug, injector, compression or wiring for that cylinder.
  4. Live data: HDS misfire counters per cylinder.
  5. Waveform: scope the trigger wire at the connector (0–5 V pulse) and the secondary with the TA398 COP probe. Compare cylinder to cylinder.

Kit items used in this worksheet

  • TA398 COP probe (secondary ignition pickup)
  • TA404 / TA405 PicoBNC+ test leads, blue (A) / red (B)
  • TA161 / TA162 flexible back-pinning probes · TA158 battery clip
Safety: Secondary voltage can exceed 20,000–40,000 volts. Never handle a coil, boot or spark tester with the engine running or cranking. Never connect a test lead directly to secondary ignition; use the TA398 COP probe. Never run a coil with the boot off a plug and no spark tester.
