Air-fuel basics

Stoichiometric: the "perfect" mixture

14.7 parts air to 1 part fuel, by weight. At this ratio all the fuel burns with all the oxygen and nothing is left over. This point is called lambda (λ) = 1.0.

  • The catalytic converter cleans HC, CO and NOx best right at λ = 1
  • So the ECM steers the mixture toward 14.7 : 1 at idle and cruise
  • Ethanol blends (E10) have a slightly lower ideal ratio, but λ = 1 still means "perfect"
14.7 lb of air
1 lb of fuel

That's roughly 9,600 L of air for every 1 L of gasoline.

Rich vs. lean

RICH · extra fuel

LEAN · extra air

Running rich

  • Black smoke, strong fuel smell
  • Sooty, fouled spark plugs
  • Poor fuel economy
  • High CO on the gas analyzer
  • Fuel trims go negative (ECM pulls fuel)
  • Common DTC: P0172

Running lean

  • Hesitation, surging, lean misfire
  • Runs hot; can damage valves and the catalyst
  • High O2 and NOx on the gas analyzer
  • Fuel trims go positive (ECM adds fuel)
  • Common DTC: P0171

Air-fuel basics

Open loop vs. closed loop

→ Open loop

The ECM uses stored fuel tables and its inputs, but doesn't check the result.

When:
  • Cold start and warm-up
  • Wide open throttle
  • Deceleration fuel cut
  • A/F sensor not ready, or a fault

↻ Closed loop

The ECM checks the exhaust with the A/F sensor and corrects the fuel. This is where fuel trims work.

When:
  • Engine warm
  • Idle and steady cruise
  • Light acceleration
  • A/F sensor heated and working
On the HDS, confirm the fuel system is in closed loop before you judge fuel trims.

Fuel trims

Short-term and long-term fuel trim

STFT · short-term

Fast, moment-to-moment correction from the A/F sensor. It moves constantly.

LTFT · long-term

Learned correction. If STFT keeps leaning one way, the ECM moves it into LTFT and remembers it.
+ positiveECM is ADDING fuel
It saw a LEAN condition
0No correction
Right on target
− negativeECM is REMOVING fuel
It saw a RICH condition
Total trim = STFT + LTFT. Some Honda HDS screens show trims as a factor around 1.00 instead of a percent: 1.10 = +10%, 0.90 = −10%.

How much trim is too much?

Removing fuel (rich)Adding fuel (lean)
−25%−10%0+10%+25%

Within about ±10%

Normal. Small everyday corrections.

About ±10% to ±25%

Something is changing the mixture. Investigate.

Beyond about ±25%

The ECM is running out of room. Expect P0171 (lean) or P0172 (rich).

Typical guidelines. Use the DTC criteria and specs in Service Express for each vehicle.

5-gas analysis

The five gases

HC
Hydrocarbons · ppm

What: unburned fuel

High: misfire, very rich or very lean

CO
Carbon monoxide · %

What: partly burned fuel

High: running rich

CO2
Carbon dioxide · %

What: a complete burn, the good result

Want it HIGH. Low means a leak, misfire or mixture problem

O2
Oxygen · %

What: leftover oxygen

High: lean, misfire or exhaust leak

NOx
Oxides of nitrogen · ppm

What: made by high combustion heat

High: lean, overheating, EGR or timing problem

5-gas analysis

Spotting the pattern

ConditionHCCOCO2O2NOxFuel trim
Rich–↑↓↓↓Negative (−)
Lean–↓↓↑↑Positive (+)
Misfire↑–↓↑↓Often positive
Exhaust leak (before sensor)––↓↑–Positive (false lean)
Quick rules: HC + O2 both high = misfire. CO high + O2 low = rich. O2 high + CO low = lean or an air leak.

Typical healthy warm idle with a working catalyst: HC under about 50 ppm, CO under about 0.5%, CO2 about 14% or more, O2 under about 1%. Follow your analyzer's and state's specs.

Wrap-up

Key takeaways

  • 14.7 : 1 (λ = 1) is the target. Rich = extra fuel; lean = extra air.
  • Inputs set the fuel (MAF/MAP, IAT, ECT, TP, CKP); the A/F sensor checks it; the fuel trim is the correction.
  • + trim = adding fuel (was lean) · − trim = pulling fuel (was rich).
  • Trims show the direction; the five gases confirm the result.
